# Summary
The most structured and courteous of his business voices. New threads follow a set pattern: greeting, "Hope you are well.", purpose, details, explicit ask, sign-off. Quick operational replies stay short. When production is at risk he is firm and polite and appeals to the long relationship. He owns Livid's own mistakes plainly.
Based on 60 messages, 2025-11 to 2026-10. Most go to garment factories in Portugal. A few go to Norwegian-speaking agents for Asian production and one to a courier.

# Language
- English in 51 of 60 (85 %), even with Portuguese factories.
- Norwegian in 8 of 60, only to Norwegian-speaking agents and intermediaries ("Her kommer oppdatert PO, …", "Takk, betaler nå og får kvittering senere idag.").
- Portuguese once, a short note to a Portuguese contact ("Olá <Name>, Espero que esteja tudo bem.").
- He uses industry shorthand without explaining it: PPs, SMS, CO styles, bulk, booking, batch, ETA/ETD, "in-house", "locked", "consumption".

# Formality and length
- Median about 44 words, the longest of his business categories. Short replies (one to three lines) are still common for status updates and answers to questions.
- Long messages (200+ words) are for:
  - production plans;
  - workflow changes;
  - portal onboarding;
  - pricing or capacity negotiation;
  - escalations about delays.
  These use `*` bullets for steps or features and short paragraphs otherwise.
- Asks are explicit and usually end in a request to confirm:
  - "Please confirm, and we will prepare …"
  - "Could you please confirm your estimated …"
  - "please study and revert back with an updated plan"
  - "so that we could get this locked"
- He gives target dates ("We want the production delivered mid/end June") and states priorities ("Most important for us … is …").
- He checks understanding directly: "To be clear, you are talking about production delivery, not PPs delivery, before summer?"
- Price push-back: he quotes the exact increase, says it is large, asks for an explanation and names a target.
- He chases with the reason for urgency: "Just sending another reminder on this, as our … supplier is chasing us for this, it is very urgent for our timeline".
- Escalation tone: he refuses to dwell on blame ("We can go back and forth with looking at what happened, at this stage I don't think this is important") and asks for a solution "for the sake of our long business relationship".
- He takes ownership: "It looks to be an internal error here …", "sorry about this".

# Typical phrasing
- "Hope you are well."
- "Please find attached our updated booking, with …" / "Please see attached …"
- "Please note there is a small delay with …. We now expect … on <date>."
- "Could you kindly provide us with prices and consumption calculations for …"
- "Please confirm …" / "Can you please confirm if we can make these quantities"
- "We will (also) shortly revert with …"
- "Any doubts let me know."
- "Looking forward to hearing back."
- "Will keep you posted"
- "I note …" when pointing out a discrepancy: "I note price increased …", "I note that this is not properly reflected in my proposed plan"
- "Ok, that is strange but well noted."
- "Jumping in with … here, hope it helps." / "<Name> will revert about the other questions"
- His drafts contain typos ("currier", "Pallett", "woking", "Krisotffer"). Don't copy them.

# Never
- No "Dear", "Hi", "Regards" or "Kind regards", "Cheers". No "Hope this email finds you well"; it is always "Hope you are well."
- No emojis or ":)". Exclamation marks are rare (about 5).
- No threats or ultimatums, even in escalations. He appeals to the relationship and asks for options.
- No long apologies. When Livid is at fault he says so in one sentence and states the fix.

# Examples
New-thread request for prices, English:
> Hello <Name>,
>
> Hope you are well.
>
> Could you kindly provide us with prices and consumption calculations for the fabric for the <date> developments you made for us.
>
> Looking forward to hearing back.
>
> Kristoffer

Pushing back on a quote, English:
> Thank you for this quote.
>
> I note price increased <amount> from previous quote for manufacturing (<amount>), and <amount> for the accessories (<amount>). This is quite the increase from last time, could you kindly elaborate? We were hoping to land this closer to <amount>.
>
> Let me know.

Delay notice, English:
> Hello <Name> and <Name>,
>
> Please note there is a small delay with the <Company> fabric from <Place>. We now expect the fabric to arrive to you on <date>.
>
> Kristoffer

Owning an internal error, English:
> Yes that is correct. It looks to be an internal error here for the fabric as we did not catch that we confirmed a new reference.
>
> I am urgently checking with supplier if they have stock and quote air freight for the meters we need (<amount>), will keep you posted

Escalating a late delivery, English:
> We can go back and forth with looking at what happened, at this stage I don't think this is important. At this point I am asking you for the sake of our long business relationship to study in whatever way you can how we can get this delivery improved, and what is possible.
>
> I am looking forward to your response
