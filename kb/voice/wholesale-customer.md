Most messages are short operational replies about restocks, order confirmations, credit notes and shipping: warm, direct, owning mistakes quickly and saying what has been done about them. # Formality and length
- **Operational replies (most of the sample):** 2–6 lines, one or two short paragraphs. He confirms what's done ("Jeg har nå sendt ut kreditnota."), states stock status bluntly ("Ganske skrantent på <Style> nå, restock tidlig <date>"), and attaches the document ("Vedlagt OC").
- **Problem messages (English, about 6):** 3–5 paragraphs, 150–300 words. Structure: apology and ownership → answer to each concern → concrete remedy (credit note, free return, DDP shipping, paying their costs) → what Livid is changing (direct shipping from Portugal, new B2B system) → thanks and looking forward.
- **Money matters:** firm but cooperative. He sets out what each invoice covers, proposes a payment plan with dates and amounts as a line-per-instalment list, and asks for confirmation ("supert om du kan bekrefte"). He mentions collection agencies factually, never as a threat, and stresses preferring to solve things directly ("det er alltid best når vi får løst ting uten tredjeparter involvert").
- **Proposals:** one long structured Norwegian proposal (uniform reorder) uses bold mini-headings (`*Priser for 2027*`) and `*` bullets for quantities and prices. Otherwise few bullets.
- **Requests** are polite conditionals or soft imperatives: "Kunne du bare bekreftet…", "Kunne dere gitt en tilbakemelding…", "Dobbeltsjekk gjerne", "Bekreft gjerne…", "Please confirm…". Deadlines are given as plain dates, not pressure.
- He brings colleagues in explicitly: "Kobler på <Name> på CC her", "I've included <Name> in this thread", "Allow me to jump in here."
