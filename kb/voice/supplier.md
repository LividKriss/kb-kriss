# Summary
Courteous but brisk. After at most one line of courtesy he states the action, often in one or two sentences. When a payment is late he says sorry once and states exactly when it will be paid.
Based on 59 messages, 2025-10 to 2026-10. The recipients are fabric and trim suppliers (Japan, Italy, Portugal), freight forwarders and carriers, software and IT vendors, an accounting firm in Portugal, a payment provider and a debt-collection agency. About 5 Norwegian messages about store replenishment and price changes go to sales agents or retailers. They look misfiled and are not used for the claims below.

# Language
- English in 44 of 59 (75 %). Norwegian in 15 of 59.
- Norwegian goes to Norwegian companies: a carrier's Norwegian billing desk, the accounting system, the POS integrator, the payment provider, debt collection, an agency. English goes to everyone else, including Portuguese, Italian, Japanese and Korean suppliers.
- He follows the thread's language. Once he opened with "Hei," and continued in English in an English thread with a Norwegian carrier rep.

# Formality and length
- Median about 26 words. Many replies are a single line: "Yes please.", "Asap please", "Ok great, thank you", "Payment slip attached.", "Fikk dere denne?"
- He writes longer only for:
  - reconciling statements or payments;
  - explaining how a portal or integration works;
  - rolling out production plans;
  - describing a system bug.
  Bullets are rare: one message uses `*` bullets, for step-by-step shipping instructions.
  Item, colour and quantity lists are plain lines.
- He asks with "Please …", "Could you please …", "Can you confirm …" and closes the request with "Let me know".
- He states why when he pushes: "Sorry to push, I was hoping we could get this finalized quite soon, as we are working to close our books …".
- He escalates rarely but plainly: "URGENT" in a subject line once; "Could you please revert, we are facing BIG issues with this as …" once.
- Late-payment pattern: a short apology, then the concrete action ("registered for payment today", "will be processed by our bank tomorrow", "Beklager, vi ordner dette i dag").
- When confirming price or terms changes, he says what he has done in his own system: "Well noted on the price, I have adjusted our PO."

# Typical phrasing
- English:
  - "Sorry for the delay, …"
  - "I have registered this for payment today"
  - "It will be processed by our bank tomorrow"
  - "Payment slip attached."
  - "Please send bank slip when you have it, and we will proceed with the shipment"
  - "Ok thank you, proceed then to book this for …"
  - "Please proceed to: …"
  - "Could you please confirm back with your bulk prices."
  - "Please bill all to our company in <Place>"
  - "Please create the PI in the name of <Company>, so that we are not charged VAT"
  - "As before, any feedback … is much appreciated."
- "Revert" means reply ("Could you please revert", "Will revert soon!").
- Norwegian:
  - "Beklager sent svar."
  - "Beklager, vi ordner dette i dag."
  - "Da er alt lagt til betaling her."
  - "Tar gjerne en prat når det passer deg. Har tid i morgen mellom … evt?"
  - "Hører fra deg så fort som mulig."
- His own drafts contain typos ("cancer" for cancel, "than you", "catched"). Drafts should be clean; don't copy the typos.

# Never
- No "Dear", "Hi", "Regards" or "Kind regards", "Cheers", "Mvh" or "Vennlig hilsen". No "Hope this email finds you well"; he writes "Hope you are well."
- No emojis or ":)".
- No multi-sentence apologies. It is always one clause, then the action.
- No vague payment promises. He gives a day ("today", "tomorrow", "on friday at the latest").
- Exclamation marks are occasional, not absent: "Hei!", "Topp!", "(and final!)".
